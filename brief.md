# Brief

Written before research begins.

## Question

In December 2025 the Oxford lexicographers crowned "rage bait" their word of the year, reporting a threefold rise in use. The internet meanwhile speaks of clickbait, flamebait, fearbait, thirst bait, correction bait, dunk bait, FOMO bait, engagement bait, and dozens more, as if these were species. They are labels at different levels: some name a target action (clicking), some an emotion (rage), some a social outcome (a flame war), some a platform objective (engagement), some a deceptive relation (bait-and-switch). Chemistry faced a vocabulary like this before 1869: a heap of named substances and no system. Is there a periodic system under the bait vocabulary, what are its elements, what would count as its Mendeleev test, and how much of that test can be run before any field data exist?

## Claim

Named baits are compounds, not elements. The paper builds the system and computes its structure. Five moves carry it:

1. **The level-mixing diagnosis, measured.** The vernacular mixes mechanisms, emotions, actions, outcomes, and ethics in one flat list, and institutional definitions do the same. The confusion is quantifiable: in the built registry, many compound pairs collide in action space (same principal targets) while no two share an element formula, so the names discriminate worse than the composition at a measured ratio.
2. **Five separated quantities instead of a bait score.** Functional bait raises an action's probability against a content-equivalent neutral framing; strategic adds selection by the sender; deceptive adds preview-payload divergence; manipulative adds bypass of reflection, operationalized as an autonomy gap between immediate and deliberated choice; harmful adds measured welfare loss. A truthful teaser and a phishing email differ on every axis but the first, which is why one scalar would erase the ethics.
3. **The architecture: elements, cues, actions, catalysts.** About 32 candidate psychological elements in 7 appraisal families (epistemic, defensive, appetitive, moral, identity, relational, status), an action alphabet of 12 (from stopping the scroll to disclosing credentials), bonds as action-specific interactions between elements, and platform conditions as catalysts that multiply exposure without creating appraisal. Emotions are intermediate states, not elements; ragebait is named after its intermediate. Cues (caps, arrows, countdowns) are surface realizations. The registry holds about 40 named compounds as sparse formulas.
4. **The computed structure.** A generative mechanism-to-action model with bonds produces each compound's response signature, and the system's periodic claims become computations: the 6 vernacular families re-emerge from behavioral signatures alone at a measured purity; the registry identifies the linear element affinities but leaves the bond matrix rank-deficient, so the existing vocabulary underdetermines the interesting chemistry, measurably; the unrealized element pairs are counted, the empty cells of the table, and ranked by predicted leverage into a list of synthesizable unnamed baits, with the seed's two predictions located on it; and a held-out compound's signature is recoverable from its formula where the family-mean baseline fails, stated honestly as internal consistency of the framework rather than empirical validation.
5. **The ecology.** Creators learn by reinforcement, users habituate, ranking amplifies by engagement. Simulated together these produce the known pathologies as regimes: engagement ranking concentrates production and tilts it toward the conflict family; habituation makes dominance cyclical, the escalation-and-churn of formats; a chronological control keeps diversity high and conflict share near its response-driven baseline. The platform is a catalyst in the exact sense: it changes yield without changing the underlying appraisals.

## Kind

**formal-model + programme**, ships a simulation. `has_simulation: true`, `claims_target: results.json`. Calibration is structural and the paper says so: the numbers are facts about the model's geometry, anchored where possible to the cited empirical record (negativity's per-word click effect, out-group sharing, moral contagion), which enters as cited records rather than fitted data.

## Constraint

The paper stands alone and cites no PIATRA paper. The Mendeleev claim is held to its test: the paper distinguishes the weak contribution (a layered ontology) from the strong one (predictive periodicity), runs internally what can be run, and specifies the preregistered factorial program for what cannot. Ethics is structural: the same registry that guides moderation guides optimization, so the dual-use discussion is a section, not a disclaimer; no deliberately inflammatory stimuli are proposed for public release. The five quantities stay separate; nothing in the paper licenses reading engagement lift as harm or as manipulation without the other measurements.

## Cornerstone literature

Each with one job:

- **Simon** — attention as the scarce resource the whole economy prices.
- **Loewenstein** — the information gap; curiosity as the first element.
- **Moors, Ellsworth, Scherer and Frijda** — appraisal theory; why elements are appraisals, not emotions.
- **Lerner and Keltner** — fear and anger diverge on certainty and control; why "negative emotion" is not an element.
- **Robertson et al.** — negativity's measured per-word click effect in 105,000 headline experiments.
- **Aubin Le Quéré and Matias** — curiosity gaps backfire; concreteness is non-monotonic.
- **Molina, Sundar, Rony and Hassan** — clickbait detectors agree 47 percent of the time; the label is not a kind.
- **Brady et al. (2017); Rathje et al.; Crockett; Brady et al. (2021)** — moral contagion, out-group sharing, outrage economics, and its social learning.
- **Lindström et al.; the 2026 hybrid model** — reward learning and habit as the creator's and user's learning loop.
- **Gray, Santos, Bielova and Mildner** — the dark-patterns ontology harmonization; the methodological precedent.
- **Reynolds and John** — ragebait as reproducible accountability entertainment; the compound in the field.
- **Wang et al.** — clicks can be cheating; exposure versus post-click satisfaction.
- **Pennycook et al.** — the accuracy nudge; interventions as element-level operations.
- **Cialdini; Brehm; Tajfel and Turner** — scarcity, proof, reciprocity, reactance, identity: the older names of several elements.
- **Luo, Sornette and Lera** — social feedback amplifying emotional language; the Hawkes-style kinetics precedent.
- **Oxford University Press; Meta** — the lexicographic and platform-policy record.
- **Mendeleev** — the standard the metaphor must meet.
