## The measured record (T1)

- **Robertson, Pröllochs, Schwarzenegger, Pärnamets et al. (2023)**, Nature Human Behaviour 7: 812–822 (10.1038/s41562-023-01538-4): in around 105,000 headline variants from the Upworthy archive, each additional negative word in an average-length headline raised click-through by about 2.3 percent. The cleanest per-element field estimate in the literature.
- **Aubin Le Quéré and Matias (2025)**, Scientific Reports 15 (10.1038/s41598-024-81575-9): headline concreteness has non-monotonic effects; maximal vagueness underperforms. Curiosity gaps are a tuned quantity, not a dial to maximize.
- **Molina, Sundar, Rony and Hassan (2021)**, CHI (10.1145/3411764.3445753): "clickbait" headlines did not reliably produce more curiosity or clicks, and 4 automated detectors agreed on classification 47 percent of the time. The label fails as a natural kind.
- **Brady, Wills, Jost, Tucker and Van Bavel (2017)**, PNAS 114: 7313–7318: moral-emotional words increase diffusion, the moral-contagion effect. **Rathje, Van Bavel and van der Linden (2021)**, PNAS 118: e2024292118: across 2.73 million posts, out-group language was the strongest predictor of sharing, roughly doubling it; out-group reference deserves its own element. **Crockett (2017)**, Nature Human Behaviour 1: 769–771: the outrage economy stated. **Brady, McLoughlin, Doan and Crockett (2021)**, Science Advances 7: eabe5641: outrage expression is socially learned from feedback, the norm-learning loop.
- **Lerner and Keltner (2001)**, JPSP 81: 146–159: fear and anger produce opposing risk judgments through certainty and control appraisals; "negative emotion" is not one mechanism.
- **Lindström et al. (2021)**, Nature Communications 12: 1311 (10.1038/s41467-020-19607-x): over a million posts and a causal experiment consistent with reward learning of posting. **Nature Communications (2026)** follow-up (10.1038/s41467-026-73547-6): in 2,696 users with a preregistered replication, a hybrid of reinforcement learning and habit describes posting best; frequent posters are more habitual.
- **Gray, Santos, Bielova and Mildner (2024)**, CHI (10.1145/3613904.3642436): ten dark-pattern taxonomies harmonized into one multilevel ontology; the methodological precedent for registry construction.
- **Reynolds and John (2026)**, Information, Communication and Society (10.1080/1369118X.2026.2665797): the Cart Narcs format as formulaic, repeatable ragebait with accountability-catharsis appeal; the compound observed in production.
- **Wang, Feng, He, Zhang et al. (2021)**, SIGIR: 1288–1297 (10.1145/3404835.3462962): counterfactual recommendation separating exposure-attractive features, clicks, and post-click satisfaction; the exposure-response decomposition in engineering practice.
- **Pennycook, Epstein, Mosleh, Arechar et al. (2021)**, Nature 592: 590–595: shifting attention to accuracy reduces misinformation sharing; interventions as appraisal-level operations.
- **Luo, Sornette and Lera (2025)**, Communications Psychology 3 (10.1038/s44271-025-00370-y): social feedback amplifies emotional language in live chats, modeled with self-exciting dynamics.
- **Oxford University Press (2025)**: "rage bait" as word of the year, December 1, 2025, usage up threefold in 12 months. **Meta (2017)**: the engagement-bait policy, defining the category through induced likes, shares, comments, and tags.

## The conceptual spine (T1)

- **Simon (1971)**, in *Computers, Communications, and the Public Interest*: a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention; the scarcity the whole system prices.
- **Loewenstein (1994)**, Psychological Bulletin 116: 75–98: curiosity as an information gap; the first and best-theorized element.
- **Moors, Ellsworth, Scherer and Frijda (2013)**, Emotion Review 5: 119–124: appraisal theories; emotions as outputs of appraisal computations, which is why the elements are appraisals and ragebait is named after an intermediate state.
- **Cialdini (1984)**, *Influence*: scarcity, social proof, reciprocity, authority, commitment, liking; several elements under their older names.
- **Brehm (1966)**, *A Theory of Psychological Reactance*: the challenge element.
- **Tajfel and Turner (1979)**, in *The Social Psychology of Intergroup Relations*: social identity; the in-group and out-group elements.
- **Mendeleev (1869)**, Zeitschrift für Chemie 12: 405–406: the property-prediction standard the metaphor must meet; the table earned its name by predicting missing elements.

## What the simulation establishes (computed, structural calibration stated in the paper)

- The registry: 32 elements in 7 families, 12 actions, 40 compounds with sparse formulas of mean size about 3; the level-mixing measured as action-space collisions against zero formula collisions.
- Family recovery: compounds clustered by model-generated response signatures alone reproduce the vernacular families at a measured purity.
- Identifiability: the linear element affinities are recoverable from the registry's signatures; the bond matrix is rank-deficient given only named compounds, so the vernacular underdetermines the chemistry; the deficit is measured.
- Empty cells: the unrealized element pairs are counted and ranked by predicted response, locating the seed's two predicted unnamed baits among the synthesizable cells.
- The ecology: engagement ranking concentrates production (higher Herfindahl index), tilts output toward the conflict family, and with habituation produces dominance cycling; the chronological control preserves diversity. The platform acts as a catalyst: yields change, appraisals do not.
